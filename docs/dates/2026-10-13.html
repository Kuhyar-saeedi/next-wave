Study guide

In one minute

REGACE is an EU project building agrivoltaic greenhouses: solar panels and crops on the same land, plus CO2 enrichment. The authors (four of six from Tor Vergata) argue that a disruptive technology only works if its end users help shape it from the first stages. In year one they ran 44 interviews with farmers in Italy, Austria, Germany, Israel and Greece, then participatory sessions combining Open Space, World Café and a fishbowl.

Key points

  • Theory: Bower and Christensen's disruptive innovation (1995). Success depends on social acceptability and contextual feasibility, not only technical performance.
  • Method: 44 in-depth interviews (30–50 minutes, in the farmers' own languages), purposive and snowball sampling, 8–15 people per session. Rigour through triangulation and member checking.
  • Session sequence: scientific input → Open Space (farmers set the topics) → World Café (rotating tables go deeper) → fishbowl (results returned).
  • Findings: farmers are open, if the technology fits local crops, climate and infrastructure. Barriers: upfront cost, maintenance, training, grid connection, bureaucracy. Opportunities: full-service contracts, links to irrigation, local energy communities.
  • Risk: without policy reform, adoption risks being limited to well-resourced farmers.

Our critique

Strong on listening and on returning results. Weaker on showing what changed in the greenhouse because of farmers: that is the professor's before/during/after test, still open in this first-year paper.

Practice questions

Why does the paper combine Open Space and World Café?
Open Space lets farmers set the agenda freely; World Café then deepens those themes in structured rounds. Participants decide the topics before experts narrow them.
What is the main limit of the study?
It reports perceptions, not yet co-design: we cannot see what changed in the technology. The sample is also small and may over-represent farmers already open to the idea.
How does this paper connect to our project?
We reuse its method sequence with tenants, add support (help, repairs, a person to call), watch for a digital version of its inequality risk, and commit to documenting the trace in the technology.
