Study guide

In one minute

The authors studied six organisations regenerating peripheral, "liminal" areas in Rome, Naples, Milan, Turin, Florence and Reggio Calabria (interviews, March–May 2022). They ask whether participation really includes vulnerable people, or only legitimises decisions taken elsewhere ("participationism"). Digital inequality is part of vulnerability: digital capital accumulates unequally.

Key points

  • Neoliberal city: citizens become service users; participation risks becoming a procedure without power.
  • Liminal space: "at the boundary of two dominant spaces, which is not fully part of either" (Dale and Burrell, 2008). Communities can turn it into a place of dwelling.
  • Digital capital (Ragnedda): skills plus technology; the most vulnerable have the least.
  • Findings: institutions are bureaucratic or absent; social capital is the engine; funding shapes participation; the pandemic pushed participation online.
  • Table 3 model: participation by invitation (top-down) or by breaking in (bottom-up), and inclusive (access) versus meaningful (real influence).
  • Tor Bella Monaca is named in the paper, compared with Arghillà and Scampia.

Our critique

A sharp theory that separates access from influence. But only organisations were interviewed, so vulnerable residents speak through intermediaries: the paper mirrors the problem it describes.

Practice questions

What is the difference between inclusive and meaningful participation?
Inclusive gives people access to the process; meaningful gives them real influence over the outcome. Our project aims for meaningful: tenants decide the fairness rule at a binding gate.
What does the paper mean for our design?
Design for low digital capital (paper, phone, a person), enter through social capital (committees, unions, parish), and avoid participationism by making tenants' choices binding.
