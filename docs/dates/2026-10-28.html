Study guide

Article 3 in one minute

A short discussion paper. Most citizen science is contributory (citizens collect data for experts), and critics call it less democratic than co-creation. The authors disagree: contributory projects can also be emancipatory. Three cases show it: CuriousNoses (20,000 people measuring NO2 in Flanders), ATRAP (25 volunteer "Snail Doctors" in Uganda) and Flip the Fleet (645 electric-car drivers in New Zealand). Their plea: a plurality of citizen sciences, chosen by the problem, not by a ranking.

  • Three meanings: contributory (Bonney), participatory (Irwin, 1995), co-creative (Shirk et al., 2012).
  • Against treating Arnstein's ladder as a ranking: not everyone has time to co-create.
  • Three ways to engage, all needed: deliberation, participation, representation.
  • Risks: data used "under the guise of opening and democratizing science", tick-box consultation, routinised labour.

Step 3: our shared question

Under what conditions can an AI energy assistant help the different households of a public housing complex share solar energy and its benefits in a way they find fair, understandable and under their control?

The developers' version was: how can an AI get tenants to shift use to sunny hours and maximise the incentive? It hid six assumptions (tenants are home by day, a bigger incentive is better, an app reaches everyone, equal is fair, data sharing is harmless, tenants are users not decision-makers).

Step 4: levels and gates

Seven phases over nine months, each with its own level. Four gates where tenants' choices bind: Gate 1 agenda (month 2), Gate 2 fairness rule and Gate 3 data charter (month 4), Gate 4 go or no-go (month 7). A wide track (every household votes at gates) and a deep track (volunteers co-design).

Practice questions

What can participants really change?
The fairness rule, the data charter, whether the assistant advises or automates, its channels, and whether the pilot continues. Developers sign the gates in advance.
Why not co-create everything?
As Article 3 argues, not everyone has the time. Everyone decides at the gates; volunteers go deeper and their proposals return to the whole community.
