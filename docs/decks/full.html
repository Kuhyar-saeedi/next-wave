Next Wave · Step 1 · presented 14 Oct

Step 1: Define the innovation problem.

4 / 69

Question 1 of 6

What is the technology, and what problem does it solve?

The problem

Families in public housing struggle to pay energy bills.

Solar panels on the roof help little: most energy is used in the evening, when there is no sun, and tenants have no say in how the money is shared.

Our technology: an AI energy assistant

1

Predicts the sun: how much energy the roof will make, hour by hour.

2

Advises families: the best time to run the washing machine or other appliances.

3

Shares the money fairly, using rules the tenants decide together.

Next Wave · Step 1 — Define the innovation problem

Problem first, then the three functions of the assistant. Stress point 3: the sharing rules are written with the tenants.

Question 2 of 6

Who develops or promotes it?

A new idea

Nobody has built this yet. Our project designs it together with the people who will use it.

It builds on

Solar forecasting and smart plugs from energy and smart-home companies, and research from universities such as Tor Vergata.

Supporters and owners

The EU, the Italian state and GSE fund energy communities. The City of Rome supports them. ATER owns the buildings.

1 · The original formulation

How the developers would frame it

"How can an AI assistant get tenants to shift their energy use to sunny hours, so the community earns the highest incentive?"

Hidden assumptions

An equal split of the money is fair

Sharing home data is unproblematic

Tenants are users, not decision-makers

Why not co-create everything?

Wide for everyone, deep for volunteers

Wide track

Every household can be informed, consulted and vote at the gates, with little time and no smartphone.

Deep track

Why these methods

Three design choices

Not survey-only

A survey can't explain why a family cooks at 9 pm. Interviews, diaries and dialogue can.

Separate, then together

Vulnerable groups speak first in their own spaces, then meet the others with their points ready.

Lottery, not volunteers

For the binding fairness decision, a mirror of the complex beats the loudest residents.

Four design rules

Digital for continuity, not to copy meetings

1

Every online step has an offline twin

Each vote, comment or survey also works on paper, by phone or in person.

2

Meet to decide, go online to continue

Decisions happen face to face; online keeps the thread between meetings.

3

Information flows both ways

Online comments are read aloud on site; on-site results are posted online within 48 hours.

4

No smartphone needed

Full participation is possible without any device, as Article 2 warns about digital capital.

Next Wave · Step 6 — Hybrid architecture

Rule 2 follows the professor's advice: use digital tools to create continuity, not simply to replicate a physical meeting online.

What citizens actually do

Observe, measure, document, classify, interpret

WIDE TRACK · ALL HOUSEHOLDS

Document: a two-week energy postcard, ticking when big appliances run.

Classify: "could I have moved this to midday? yes / no / why".

Consent: optional sharing of 15-minute meter data.

DEEP TRACK · ~30 VOLUNTEERS

Measure: smart plugs on the washing machine or water heater.

Observe: photos of roof shade at fixed times.

Interpret: label data, compare it with the AI forecast, propose changes.

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

The two tracks come from Article 3: wide, low-threshold contribution gives scale and reaches busy people; the deep track keeps citizens in interpretation and avoids the extractive model.

Credible enough for ATER and GSE to listen

Who defines the protocol

Tenants and researchers together, then a 5-household pilot to fix unclear steps.

Cross-checks

Postcards and diaries compared with meter data; mismatches discussed, not deleted.

A 30-minute session, a picture guide and an ambassador to call.

48 / 69

Avoiding the extractive model

Tenants are not unpaid sensors

Data belongs to the CER

The provider only processes it, under the tenants' data charter.

Privacy by design

Aggregated by default, deleted after the pilot, GDPR consent per use.

Opt out, no penalty

Not sharing data never reduces a household's share.

Results returned

Each household gets its own "energy postcard" in plain language.

Right to challenge

Any finding can be contested at a data evening and re-checked.

Recognition

Named credit, certificates and a small allowance for volunteers.

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

These six points answer the professor's slide "avoid the extractive model": data ownership, privacy and future use, recognition of local knowledge, returning results in understandable forms, and a mechanism to challenge findings.

From a postcard to a change in the AI

1 · DATA

Postcards, plugs and photos collected

2 · DATA EVENING

3 · PROPOSAL

4 · DECISION

5 · CHANGE LOG

Public record: accepted, changed, or refused with reasons

What it is

Make the fairness choice tangible

The problem it solves

"Fair sharing" is abstract. Tenants can't vote on a formula they can't see working.

The simulator shows, family by family, what each rule would mean in euros.

How it works

1. Pick a sunny or cloudy month.

2. Mix the fairness rules with sliders.

3. See each household's share change live, and why.

Next Wave · Step 8 — Prototype

It uses simulated households and approximate solar figures, clearly labelled as such. It is a deliberation tool, not the final AI system.

52 / 69

The rules tenants can mix

Four ideas of what is fair

Equal

Every household gets the same amount.

Simple, but ignores need and effort.

Need

More for low-income or larger households.

Fights energy poverty, needs sensitive data.

Effort

More for those who use energy in sunny hours.

Raises the bonus, penalises shift workers.

Floor

A minimum guaranteed to everyone, whatever the mix.

Protects those who can't shift.

Tenants set the weight of each rule; the simulator shows the trade-offs. The final mix is decided at Gate 2.

Next Wave · Step 8 — Prototype

These are the options the tenant voices in Step 3 pointed to: equality, need (the unions), effort (the developers' logic) and protection (shift workers and the elderly).

What testing teaches us

Questions the prototype answers

Which rule do tenants prefer?

And does the preference change once they see the effect on others?

Is the algorithm explainable?

If tenants can't explain it, the final AI must be simpler.

What data are tenants willing to share?

The answer shapes the data charter (Gate 3).

Does paper work as well as screen?

If not, the hybrid design needs more support.

3 · Power balance

Experts on tap, not on top

Neutral facilitators

Not from ATER or the provider; trained in conflict and inclusion.

Tenants speak first

Experts answer questions; they don't open discussions.

No-jargon rule

Anyone can raise a "say it simply" card at any time.

Tenants hold the vote

On fairness and data, only tenants vote; institutions give opinions.

Next Wave · Step 9 — Facilitation and inclusion

4 · Conflict

Disagreement is recorded, not hidden

1 · NAME IT

The facilitator writes each disagreement in a public log

2 · EXPLORE IT

Fishbowl: both sides explain their reasons

3 · TEST IT

Simulator or pilot data where facts can help

4 · DECIDE IT

Vote at the gate; minority views attached to the decision

IF TENANTS DISAGREE WITH THE DEVELOPERS

Gates are binding: the provider adapts, or the pilot is paused. An external mediator (e.g. the university) is called before any override.

5 · Traceability · 6 · Feedback

Every contribution can be followed

Traceability

Each proposal gets a number, on paper and online.

Its status is public: received, discussed, adopted, changed or refused, with reasons.

Feedback

Within 48 hours: session results posted on both channels.

Monthly: "you said, we did" bulletin and a courtyard return event.

End of pilot: each household's energy postcard.

The unresolved dilemma

Fairness versus a bigger bonus

The tension

Rewarding effort raises the community's total income. Protecting those who can't shift lowers it. Tenants may choose a fairer but smaller pie.

The legal limit

Tenants without a regular contract may not be able to join the CER. Participation can include them in the discussion, but not, alone, in the benefits.

We accept both as open questions, and our process makes them visible instead of hiding them in code.

Next Wave · Step 10 — Evaluation framework

The final pitch includes one minute on a key risk or unresolved dilemma. This is ours. Whether irregular occupants can join depends on CER rules and ATER policy; we flag it as a question, not a fact.

The answer to the course question

What will be different because people participated?

The rule

The money would be shared by a fairness rule tenants chose and voted on, not one written by the provider.

The data

The AI would learn from tenant-collected knowledge about real daily life, under data rules tenants agreed.

The access

Elders, shift workers and non-Italian speakers could use and control the assistant without a smartphone.

Participation did not decorate the technology. It is designed to change it.
