Question 1 of 6

What is the technology, and what problem does it solve?

The problem

Families in public housing struggle to pay energy bills.

Solar panels on the roof help little: most energy is used in the evening, when there is no sun, and tenants have no say in how the money is shared.

Our technology: an AI energy assistant

1

Predicts the sun: how much energy the roof will make, hour by hour.

2

Advises families: the best time to run the washing machine or other appliances.

3

Shares the money fairly, using rules the tenants decide together.

7 / 12

What citizens actually do

Observe, measure, document, classify, interpret

WIDE TRACK · ALL HOUSEHOLDS

Document: a two-week energy postcard, ticking when big appliances run.

Classify: "could I have moved this to midday? yes / no / why".

Consent: optional sharing of 15-minute meter data.

DEEP TRACK · ~30 VOLUNTEERS

Measure: smart plugs on the washing machine or water heater.

Observe: photos of roof shade at fixed times.

Interpret: label data, compare it with the AI forecast, propose changes.

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

The two tracks come from Article 3: wide, low-threshold contribution gives scale and reaches busy people; the deep track keeps citizens in interpretation and avoids the extractive model.

8 / 12

The rules tenants can mix

Four ideas of what is fair

Equal

Every household gets the same amount.

Simple, but ignores need and effort.

Need

More for low-income or larger households.

Fights energy poverty, needs sensitive data.

Effort

More for those who use energy in sunny hours.

Raises the bonus, penalises shift workers.

Floor

A minimum guaranteed to everyone, whatever the mix.

Protects those who can't shift.

Tenants set the weight of each rule; the simulator shows the trade-offs. The final mix is decided at Gate 2.

Next Wave · Step 8 — Prototype

These are the options the tenant voices in Step 3 pointed to: equality, need (the unions), effort (the developers' logic) and protection (shift workers and the elderly).

11 / 12

The unresolved dilemma

Fairness versus a bigger bonus

The tension

Rewarding effort raises the community's total income. Protecting those who can't shift lowers it. Tenants may choose a fairer but smaller pie.

The legal limit

Tenants without a regular contract may not be able to join the CER. Participation can include them in the discussion, but not, alone, in the benefits.

We accept both as open questions, and our process makes them visible instead of hiding them in code.
