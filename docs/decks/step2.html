Who is involved in an AI energy assistant for a solar energy community in Rome's public housing, and who is missing

Mahsa Shahi · Kuhyar Saeedi · Farida Safdari · Gelila Tsehaye Melesse · Md Nazmul Islam · Pranto Argha Sarker

Reminder of Step 1 in one line: an AI assistant that helps a public housing complex use its rooftop solar energy and shares the money by rules the tenants decide. Step 2 asks: who is affected, who has power, who knows what, and who is missing.

2 / 10

1 · Actors

Who is involved?

Affected and users

Tenant families, elderly tenants, migrant families, day and night-shift workers, people with disabilities

Develop and build

AI tech provider, solar installer, Tor Vergata researchers

Own and manage

ATER Roma (owns the buildings), the CER association, Areti (Rome's grid operator)

Regulate

ARERA (energy regulator), GSE (incentive rules), Garante Privacy (data protection)

Fund and support

EU and Italian state, GSE incentive, City of Rome and Municipio VI, third-sector groups and the parish

May oppose or doubt

Distrustful tenants, tenant unions (e.g. SUNIA, Unione Inquilini), privacy advocates

3 · Knowledge

Who knows what?

Scientific and technical

Researchers and the tech provider: forecasting, AI, solar systems

Institutional

GSE, ARERA, ATER, the City: rules, incentives, permits

Practical

Installer and Areti: roofs, the grid, meter data

Local and experiential

Tenants, committees, social workers: daily life in the buildings

Only tenants know what the AI can't measure:

their daily routines, who has no smartphone, which flats are empty during the day, and the promises that were broken before.

5 · Vulnerability

Who could be harmed or left out?

Elderly living alone

Barrier: no smartphone, low digital skills

Risk: miss the benefits, lose control to automation

Low-income families

Barrier: no time, can't buy smart devices

Risk: carry costs, gain little

Migrant families

Barrier: language, distrust of institutions

Risk: not informed, not represented

Shift and day workers

Barrier: away when the sun shines, no time for meetings

Risk: the algorithm rewards people who stay home

People with disabilities

Barrier: inaccessible meetings and apps

Risk: automation touching devices they depend on

Tenants without a contract

Barrier: may not be allowed to join the CER

Risk: excluded completely

What the map tells us

Three lessons for our process

1

Closest, but weakest

The people closest to the innovation have the least power over it. Our process must shift that balance.

2

Data is the conflict

The provider and the grid operator want data. Tenants fear losing privacy and control. Rules must be agreed together.

3

Trusted bridges

The committee, unions, charities and the parish can reach the missing voices that institutions can't.

Next, Step 3: rewrite the problem together with these actors.
