Step 3 asks: write the original problem formulation, imagine what different actors would add or contest, then rewrite it as a shared participatory question.

2 / 6

1 · The original formulation

How the developers would frame it

"How can an AI assistant get tenants to shift their energy use to sunny hours, so the community earns the highest incentive?"

Hidden assumptions

Tenants are flexible and at home in the day

A bigger incentive means a better project

An app is enough to reach everyone

An equal split of the money is fair

Sharing home data is unproblematic

Tenants are users, not decision-makers

Next Wave · Step 3 — Co-define the participatory challenge

This is the typical top-down framing: the problem is "behaviour change" and the tenants are the variable to optimise. Each hidden assumption is something tenants could contest.

"I don't use apps. Who explains this to me, and can I say no?"

3 · How the problem shifts

From optimising tenants to designing with them

Collect all useful dataA data charter tenants approve
Automate appliancesAdvise by default, automate only by choice

4 · Our shared participatory question

Under what conditions can an AI energy assistant help the different households of a public housing complex share solar energy and its benefits in a way they find fair, understandable and under their control?

Next Wave · Step 3 — Co-define the participatory challenge

Compare with the professor's agrivoltaics example: not "how do we convince farmers" but "under what conditions can it become useful and workable". Our question also opens the possibility that, for some households, the answer is "it can't", which is part of a real participatory process.

6 / 6

5 · Five questions we answer together

What tenants will decide

Fairness: how should the money be shared between households?

Data: what can the AI know, who sees it, how long is it kept?

Control: should the assistant advise, or also switch appliances?

Access: how do people without a smartphone take part fully?

Governance: who runs the community, and how can decisions be challenged?
