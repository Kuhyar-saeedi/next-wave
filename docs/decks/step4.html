The five levels, for our project

What each level means for tenants

1 · INFORM

How a CER and the AI work; tenants' rights

2 · CONSULT

Routines, needs, fears, what "fair" means to each household

3 · INVOLVE

Tenants set the agenda for what gets co-designed

4 · CO-DESIGN

Fairness rule, data charter, the assistant's messages and channels

5 · CO-CREATE

Knowledge from citizen science; governance of the community

Decision points

Four moments where tenants' choices bind

GATE 1 · MONTH 2

The agenda

Tenants vote on which issues are co-designed first.

GATE 2 · MONTH 4

Fairness rule

The tenant assembly adopts the rule; the provider must implement it.

GATE 3 · MONTH 4

Data charter

No household data is used beyond what the charter allows.

GATE 4 · MONTH 7

Go or no-go

After the pilot, tenants and ATER decide to scale, change or stop.

Our commitment: developers sign these gates in advance, so participation cannot be ignored afterwards.

Next Wave · Step 4 — Levels of participation

This answers the professor's question "What can participants really change?" Each gate names a decision that changes the technology or its governance. A no-go is a legitimate outcome.

Why not co-create everything?

Wide for everyone, deep for volunteers

Wide track

Every household can be informed, consulted and vote at the gates, with little time and no smartphone.

Deep track

A rotating group of volunteers co-designs and co-creates, with childcare, flexible times and recognition.

As Article 3 argues, not everyone has the time to co-create, but everyone should be able to decide at the gates.

Next Wave · Step 4 — Levels of participation

The deep track rotates so that it does not become a closed group of the usual participants. Its proposals always go back to the wide track for a decision.
