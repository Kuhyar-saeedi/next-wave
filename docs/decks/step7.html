Research questions

Three things we need to know

RQ1

When do households really use energy, and what stops them from using it in sunny hours?

RQ2

Where does the AI forecast go wrong, and for which kinds of households?

RQ3

How would each fairness rule change what real households receive?

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

RQ1 is about routines and barriers, RQ2 about bias in the AI, RQ3 about the consequences of the fairness choice. Each answer goes to a specific decision: RQ1 to the assistant's design, RQ2 to the model, RQ3 to the fairness assembly.

From question to change

QuestionRQ1–RQ3: routines, forecast errors, effects of fairness rules

The professor's six-step model: question, protocol, participation, validation, interpretation, feedback. The key point is that citizens are present in the protocol and the interpretation, not only in data collection.

What citizens actually do

Observe, measure, document, classify, interpret

WIDE TRACK · ALL HOUSEHOLDS

Document: a two-week energy postcard, ticking when big appliances run.

Classify: "could I have moved this to midday? yes / no / why".

Consent: optional sharing of 15-minute meter data.

DEEP TRACK · ~30 VOLUNTEERS

Measure: smart plugs on the washing machine or water heater.

Observe: photos of roof shade at fixed times.

Interpret: label data, compare it with the AI forecast, propose changes.

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

The two tracks come from Article 3: wide, low-threshold contribution gives scale and reaches busy people; the deep track keeps citizens in interpretation and avoids the extractive model.

5 / 7

Data quality and uncertainty

Credible enough for ATER and GSE to listen

Who defines the protocol

Tenants and researchers together, then a 5-household pilot to fix unclear steps.

Cross-checks

Postcards and diaries compared with meter data; mismatches discussed, not deleted.

Training and support

A 30-minute session, a picture guide and an ambassador to call.

Honest uncertainty

Every chart shows how sure we are; small samples are labelled as such.

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

From Article 3 (CuriousNoses): validated protocols made citizen data impossible to dismiss. We aim for the same credibility with the housing authority and the energy institutions.

6 / 7

Avoiding the extractive model

Tenants are not unpaid sensors

Data belongs to the CER

The provider only processes it, under the tenants' data charter.

Privacy by design

Aggregated by default, deleted after the pilot, GDPR consent per use.

Opt out, no penalty

Not sharing data never reduces a household's share.

Results returned

Each household gets its own "energy postcard" in plain language.

Right to challenge

Any finding can be contested at a data evening and re-checked.

Recognition

Named credit, certificates and a small allowance for volunteers.

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

These six points answer the professor's slide "avoid the extractive model": data ownership, privacy and future use, recognition of local knowledge, returning results in understandable forms, and a mechanism to challenge findings.

7 / 7

How knowledge enters decisions

From a postcard to a change in the AI

1 · DATA

Postcards, plugs and photos collected

2 · DATA EVENING

Tenants and researchers interpret together

3 · PROPOSAL

A written change request with evidence

4 · DECISION

Assembly or provider must answer within a month

5 · CHANGE LOG

Public record: accepted, changed, or refused with reasons

EXAMPLE

Diaries show shift workers can't shift laundry to noon → the assembly adds a "flexibility credit" for night-time water-heater timers → the AI learns a new household type.

Next Wave · Step 7 — Citizen science

The example is illustrative, a plausible outcome, not a result. The professor will ask "How does citizen-generated knowledge enter a decision?": this five-step path, with a duty to answer, is our answer.
