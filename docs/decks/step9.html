2 · Accessibility

Remove the barriers before people meet them

Language

Plain Italian, key materials translated, mediators at sessions

Timing

Each activity offered twice: weekday evening and Saturday morning

Mobility

Ground-floor rooms in the complex; home visits if needed

Disability

Large print, audio versions, accessible routes and seating

Digital access

Every step works on paper, phone or in person (Step 6)

Care and cost

Childcare, snacks, and a small voucher for long sessions

Covers the professor's five accessibility dimensions: language, timing, mobility, disability, digital access, plus care and cost, which often decide whether low-income tenants can come at all.

3 · Power balance

Experts on tap, not on top

Neutral facilitators

Not from ATER or the provider; trained in conflict and inclusion.

Tenants speak first

Experts answer questions; they don't open discussions.

No-jargon rule

Anyone can raise a "say it simply" card at any time.

Tenants hold the vote

On fairness and data, only tenants vote; institutions give opinions.

Next Wave · Step 9 — Facilitation and inclusion

Plus small groups and speaking tokens so dominant residents cannot monopolise the room. Article 2 warns that facilitators can become a "technocracy" of participation: facilitators are evaluated by participants after each session.

4 · Conflict

Disagreement is recorded, not hidden

1 · NAME IT

The facilitator writes each disagreement in a public log

2 · EXPLORE IT

Fishbowl: both sides explain their reasons

3 · TEST IT

Simulator or pilot data where facts can help

4 · DECIDE IT

Vote at the gate; minority views attached to the decision

IF TENANTS DISAGREE WITH THE DEVELOPERS

Gates are binding: the provider adapts, or the pilot is paused. An external mediator (e.g. the university) is called before any override.

Answer to the professor's question: "What happens if stakeholders disagree with the developers?" The developer does not get the last word on fairness or data.

5 · Traceability · 6 · Feedback

Every contribution can be followed

Traceability

Each proposal gets a number, on paper and online.

Its status is public: received, discussed, adopted, changed or refused, with reasons.

Feedback

Within 48 hours: session results posted on both channels.

Monthly: "you said, we did" bulletin and a courtyard return event.

End of pilot: each household's energy postcard.
