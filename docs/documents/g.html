G · Evaluation Framework

Deliverable G

Deliverable G, Step 10 · Hybrid Participation Innovation Lab, Tor Vergata · Next Wave: Mahsa Shahi, Kuhyar Saeedi, Farida Safdari, Gelila Tsehaye Melesse, Md Nazmul Islam, Pranto Argha Sarker.

We judge the process not by how many people came, but by what changed in the AI energy assistant because they came. Headcount and satisfaction are recorded but are not the main measures.

1. Six dimensions

Dimension Indicator How we measure it
Inclusion Participants compared with the complex by age, origin, household type and building Sign-in sheets, assembly lottery records
Dialogue Share of speaking time by tenants; disagreements logged and answered Facilitator logs, disagreement log
Knowledge Citizen data used in the model, the messages or the fairness rule Change-log references to citizen findings
Learning Understanding of the CER and the AI, before vs after Short quiz and interviews at start and end
Innovation Features, rules or governance changed by participation Before/after comparison of the design
Continuity Active monitoring group and ambassadors after the pilot Attendance 3 and 6 months later

2. Before, during, after: documenting the trace

  1. Before. We freeze the developers' starting design, signed and dated: an incentive-maximising AI, an equal split, full automation, app only, and the provider holding the data.
  2. During. The change log records every proposal, data finding, controversy, prototype test and rejected alternative.
  3. After. We compare the final design with the frozen one, line by line, and trace each change back to a contribution.

Freezing the starting design is what makes change measurable.

3. What participation could change in the technology

The right-hand column lists possible outcomes, not promises: tenants may decide differently. What matters is that each line could change, and that we record why it did or did not.

Element Before (developers) Possible after (participation)
Fairness Equal split Mixed rule with a minimum floor for all
Control Automates appliances Advises by default; automation opt-in
Access App only App, SMS, paper calendar, phone line
Data Provider holds all data CER owns data under a tenant charter
Model Generic household profiles New types learned from diaries (e.g. shift work)
Governance Board of ATER and provider Elected tenant seats and an appeals route

4. Evidence of success, and the open dilemma

Participation worked if:

  • at least one design element changed and can be traced to tenants' input;
  • the fairness assembly mirrored the complex, not only the usual participants;
  • vulnerable households' share was not lower than under the developers' default;
  • tenants can explain the rule that shares their money;
  • a tenant group keeps monitoring after the pilot ends.

The unresolved dilemma. Rewarding effort raises the community's total income; protecting those who cannot shift their use lowers it. Tenants may choose a fairer but smaller pie. A second limit is legal: tenants without a regular contract may not be able to join the CER. Participation can include them in the discussion but not, on its own, in the benefits; whether they can join depends on CER rules and ATER policy. We treat both as open questions, and our process makes them visible instead of hiding them in code.
