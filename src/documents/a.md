# A · Context Brief: an AI energy assistant for a solar energy community

Deliverable A, Step 1 · Hybrid Participation Innovation Lab, Tor Vergata · Group **Next Wave**: Mahsa Shahi, Kuhyar Saeedi, Farida Safdari, Gelila Tsehaye Melesse, Md Nazmul Islam, Pranto Argha Sarker.

We propose an AI energy assistant for a solar Renewable Energy Community (CER) in a public housing complex in Rome's periphery, with the rule that shares its money co-designed by the tenants.

## 1. The technology and the problem it solves

Families in public housing struggle to pay their energy bills. Solar panels on the roof help little: most household energy is used in the evening, when there is no sun, and tenants have no say in how the community's money is shared.

Our innovation is an AI energy assistant for a solar energy community (CER). It does three things:

1. **Predicts the sun.** It forecasts how much energy the roof will produce, hour by hour.
2. **Advises families.** It tells each household the best time to run the washing machine, water heater or other big appliances.
3. **Shares the money fairly.** The CER earns an incentive for energy shared inside the community. The assistant splits it between households using a fairness rule the tenants decide together.

The third function is what makes it new. Existing tools forecast and advise; none lets the residents themselves write the rule the algorithm uses to share money.

## 2. Who develops or promotes it

Nobody has built this assistant yet; our project designs it together with the people who will use it. It builds on existing work: solar forecasting and smart plugs from energy and smart-home companies, and energy-community research at universities such as Tor Vergata.

| Actor | Role |
| --- | --- |
| EU and Italian state, GSE | Fund and regulate energy communities; GSE pays the incentive |
| City of Rome (Roma Capitale) | Promotes solidarity energy communities in public housing |
| ATER Roma | Owns the buildings and roofs |
| AI technology provider | Would build and run the assistant |
| Tenants and their committee | Future users and members of the CER |

## 3. Where it will be implemented

A pilot in one public housing complex in Rome's eastern periphery, for example Tor Bella Monaca (Municipio VI). We chose this kind of site for three reasons:

- **Large flat roofs** with space for solar panels that today produce nothing.
- **Many low-income households** who struggle with energy bills.
- **Close to Tor Vergata University**, a possible research partner for on-site workshops.

## 4. Expected benefits

- **Lower energy costs.** More solar energy used at the right time means a bigger incentive for the community to share.
- **Fair sharing.** The money is shared by a rule the tenants themselves agree is fair.
- **Energy know-how.** Families learn when and how to use energy better.
- **Cleaner energy.** More local solar power, less CO2 and air pollution.

## 5. Uncertainties, risks and controversies

| Issue | What could go wrong |
| --- | --- |
| Fairness | People disagree on what a fair share is: equal, by need, or by effort |
| Privacy | The AI needs data on when people are at home; who sees it? |
| Trust | Tenants may not trust a computer deciding about money |
| Exclusion | Elderly people and those without smartphones may be left out |
| Control | Should the assistant only advise, or also switch appliances on? |
| Costs | It is unclear who pays for devices, maintenance and repairs |
| Eligibility | Tenants without a regular contract may not be able to join the CER |

## 6. Why participation is necessary

The assistant decides who gets money, so the tenants must help write its rules. Three questions cannot be answered by developers alone:

- **What is fair?** Equal shares, more for poorer families, or more for those who shift their use to sunny hours?
- **What data?** What the AI may know about each home, who can see it, and for how long.
- **Advise or control?** Whether the assistant only suggests, or may also switch appliances on.

Tenants know their routines and needs best. Without them, nobody trusts or uses the system, and the most vulnerable households are the first to be left out.
