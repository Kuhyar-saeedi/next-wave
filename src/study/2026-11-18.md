## In one minute

"Energy Detectives" produces the knowledge the AI is missing. Three research questions: when households really use energy and what stops them using it in sunny hours (RQ1); where the forecast goes wrong (RQ2); how each fairness rule changes what real households receive (RQ3). Citizens help write the protocol and interpret the data, and findings must receive an answer.

## Key points

- **Wide track (all households):** two-week energy postcard, classify each use, optional meter-data consent.
- **Deep track (about 30 volunteers):** smart plugs, roof-shade photos, labelling data and comparing it with the forecast.
- **Quality:** co-written protocol, 5-household pilot, cross-checks with meter data, honest uncertainty.
- **From data to decision:** data → data evening → written proposal → answer within a month → public change log.
- **Ethics:** data belongs to the CER, privacy by design, opt-out without penalty, right to challenge, recognition.

## Practice questions

### Q: How does citizen-generated knowledge enter a decision?
Through a five-step path with a duty to answer: tenants and researchers interpret the data together, write a change request, and the assembly or provider must answer within a month in a public log.

### Q: How do you avoid treating tenants as unpaid sensors?
They co-write the protocol, interpret the results, own the data through the CER, can opt out without losing money, and receive recognition and their own energy postcard.
